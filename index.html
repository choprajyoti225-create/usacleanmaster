The Long Flight of the Monarch

Every autumn, millions of monarch butterflies leave Canada and the northern United States and fly as far as 4,000 kilometres south to a handful of misty fir forests high in the mountains of central Mexico — a place none of them has ever seen. The butterflies that make this journey are the great-great-grandchildren of the ones that left the year before, yet they find the same mountainsides, sometimes the very same trees, guided by the angle of the sun and an internal compass scientists are still working to understand. There they cluster by the thousands on the branches, so densely that the boughs bend under their weight, waiting out the winter until the warming days of spring send them north again to begin the cycle anew.
